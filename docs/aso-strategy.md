# Metronome ASO and Store Strategy

Last updated: 2026-08-31

## Product Position

Metronome is a precise, distraction-free metronome with serious practice tools for musicians of all levels practising independently. Tempo Trainer is one capability, not the product's defining purpose. The store presentation must lead with reliable timing and better practice, then prove that position with the broader toolkit.

## Current Store Findings

### Apple

The current listing uses **Metronome: Feel the Beat!**, the subtitle **Metronome for musicians**, and Utilities as its primary category. It has three screenshots, English metadata only and no displayed rating summary. The current description omits several shipped practice capabilities. The live listing is [App Store ID 6761737690](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/metronome-feel-the-beat/id6761737690).

Apple permits a 30-character name, a 30-character subtitle, a 100-character keyword field and up to ten screenshots. Its guidance recommends a distinctive name, specific non-duplicated keywords and benefit-led screenshots. See [Creating your product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/) and [screenshot requirements](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/upload-app-previews-and-screenshots).

A directional scan of Apple's public Australian search endpoint did not return this app in the first 50 results for metronome, practice metronome, tempo trainer, gap trainer metronome, offline metronome or BPM metronome. This endpoint is not an authoritative App Store rank, but the absence supports improving category, metadata, ratings and conversion assets together.

### Google Play

The current listing has 1K+ downloads but no visible rating. Its short description is **Set your rhythm with style**. The full description predates the shipped practice toolkit and contains the phrase **will hold you back as you play**, which reverses the intended claim. The live listing is [Google Play package com.merkost.metronome](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.merkost.metronome).

Google permits a 30-character title and an 80-character short description. Its guidance favours accurate, succinct, natural copy and prohibits misleading claims or repetitive keyword blocks. See [Google Play store listing guidance](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723?hl=en).

## User Review Evidence

### First-party evidence

- Apple review feeds returned zero written reviews across Australia, United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Japan, Brazil, Mexico and India on 2026-08-31.
- The live Australian App Store listing says there are not enough ratings or reviews to display an overview.
- The live Google Play listing shows 1K+ downloads without a visible aggregate rating or review section.
- A stale third-party AppBrain snapshot recorded one positive comment about the basic metronome and its UI, but it predates the current product and is not sufficient evidence for a marketing claim. See the [historical AppBrain page](https://www.appbrain.com/app/metronome-feel-the-beat/com.merkost.metronome).

There is therefore no meaningful first-party review corpus to score or quote. No testimonial, rating claim or review-derived product claim should be published yet.

### Directional category evidence

Reviews of adjacent metronome apps consistently value:

- fast, understandable tempo controls;
- stable audible timing and sufficient volume;
- background playback while reading music or using another app;
- gradual tempo training for difficult passages;
- an ad-free experience without persistent subscription prompts;
- useful depth that does not make the basic metronome harder to operate.

Common frustrations include unreliable sound, excessive monetisation prompts, complexity, weak paid-value differentiation and missing session conveniences. These patterns are visible in current reviews for [Tack](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=xyz.zedler.patrick.tack), [Metronome Beats](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.andymstone.metronome), [Soundbrenner](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.soundbrenner.pulse) and [NixGame Metronome](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.nixgame.metronome). They are directional category evidence, not reviews of this product.

## Native Review Request Policy

The app should use the native App Store and Google Play review sheets. It must not show a custom sentiment gate, request a five-star rating, ask whether the user likes the app, or show a success or failure message after requesting a review.

The first request becomes eligible only when all of these are true:

1. Cumulative metronome playback in the current app process has reached at least 300,000 milliseconds.
2. Playback has transitioned from playing to paused.
3. Playback remains paused for two seconds.
4. No countdown timer is configured, including a paused or completed timer that has not been dismissed.
5. No Tempo Trainer or Gap Trainer remains active.
6. Neither the timer sheet nor tempo sheet is open.
7. Onboarding coach marks are not visible.
8. The app is foregrounded and a native presentation host is available.
9. The current app version has not already requested a review.
10. At least 180 days have passed since the preceding request attempt, if one exists.

The request is cancelled when playback resumes during the two-second delay. If an active timer or trainer blocks the request, the app waits for a later qualified pause rather than presenting immediately when that mode is dismissed.

The request attempt is persisted only after a foreground native host is available and the platform request pipeline starts. The operating system or store may still suppress the sheet, and the app must not infer whether the person rated it. The existing manual **Rate the App** Settings action remains available.

Apple recommends prompting after a satisfying completed action, delaying briefly to avoid interruption, and avoiding direct-button use of the system prompt. Google similarly recommends waiting until the person has experienced enough of the product and forbids custom questions around the native card. See [Apple review guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/StoreKit/requesting-app-store-reviews) and [Google Play In-App Reviews guidance](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/in-app-review).

## Screenshot Narrative

Replace the current three version-1.0-era screenshots with this eight-frame sequence:

1. **Precise timing. Better practice.** — active main metronome.
2. **Build speed automatically** — Tempo Trainer configuration and progress.
3. **Train your inner clock** — Gap Trainer audible and silent phases.
4. **Hear every subdivision** — eighths, triplets and sixteenths.
5. **Shape every beat** — accent and mute controls.
6. **Save tempos. Track progress.** — saved tempos, practice time and streak.
7. **Keep practising from the Lock Screen** — iOS Live Activity and Dynamic Island; use background playback and notification controls on Android.
8. **No ads. No account. Core tools offline.** — restrained closing proof frame.

The first three frames must carry the core promise and the two most differentiated practice benefits because Apple can show those frames directly in search results.

## Icon Strategy

Keep the current icon as the control and test two more distinctive alternatives:

1. A mechanical pendulum silhouette using one recognisable app colour.
2. A strong pulse or beat mark that remains legible at small sizes.

Avoid text, excessive detail and marks that can be mistaken for a letter. Apple product-page optimisation supports up to three icon, screenshot or preview treatments; Google Play supports graphical and localised listing experiments. See [Apple product-page optimisation](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/create-product-page-optimization-tests/overview-of-product-page-optimization) and [Google Play experiments](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12053285?hl=en).
